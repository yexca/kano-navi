# LINE BLOG Archive

The Line Blog page brings back Kano's LINE BLOG from 2015 to 2019: 99 articles in
their original Japanese, with the photos, pictographs, and videos they were
posted with. It is an unofficial archive built mainly on a fan's recovery of
the blog from web archive captures, credited on the About page, and laid out
like the original blog with its own header art, menu bar and profile photos.
The blog changed its design a few times; each page shows the design the blog
had when its articles were posted. The design picker at the right of the menu
bar keeps one of the blog's designs, with its header art and profile photo,
on every page instead; **Auto** returns to the design of the articles shown.
The choice is remembered on this device.

## Reading

The front page lists the newest articles three at a time, with page numbers
at the bottom. Open an article's title to read it on its own. Under it, the
blog's own buttons lead to the older article (前の記事) and the newer one
(次の記事); pointing at a button shows that article's title. The sidebar
shows the blog's profile, the ten most recent articles, and a monthly archive
grouped by year. Dates and times are in Japan time, as the blog showed them.

Select a photo to view it in full; arrow keys move between the article's
photos. Small print at the bottom names the site the picture was recovered
from; most of those original addresses no longer open. Videos that were embedded from YouTube play from the archived copy,
with a link to the original on YouTube.

## On Phones

On a phone, or in a window up to about 600 pixels wide, the page takes the
look of LINE BLOG's own smartphone pages instead: the header art cut to its
title and picture, a menu bar that scrolls sideways with ホーム and
鹿乃オフィシャルサイト, the **AI** mark and the design picker, and a one-line
profile that opens the full profile with its photo. The front page lists
three articles at a time, each with its title, picture, opening words and
date, and the round buttons at the bottom go to the first, previous, next and
last pages. An article ends with its comment count, buttons to the older
article (前の記事), the front page (ホーム) and the newer article (次の記事),
and its comments. A pull-down under every page opens any month. Kano's own
phone header and the blog's phone article pages were never archived, so
these pages follow the template the blog shared with other LINE BLOGs.

## Comments

Under an article, its comment list shows what readers, and sometimes Kano,
wrote: each comment's number, the name its writer gave, the date and time in
Japan time, and its text, newest first, with the star or face rating that
early comments carried. Comments stay in their original Japanese and are not
translated. The links commenters left to their own blogs are not kept.

## Translation

With Simplified Chinese or English as the content language (see
[Pages and Settings](index.md#pages-and-settings)), each paragraph is followed
by its translation into that language; Japanese shows the original only. The menu
bar carries the same **AI** mark as translated video titles: select it to
hide the translations of every article, and select **Original** to show them
again; the choice is remembered on this device. The same mark on an
article's date line switches that article alone, until the menu bar's mark
is used again. Both translations come with the recovered archive and are AI
translations of the Japanese posts.

## What Was Not Preserved

Share counts and some images were not captured; two pictures of one 2017
article appear as a note with a link to the original address instead.
The store thumbnails beside product links were recovered from archived copies
of their original addresses. Where an original picture was lost but the same
official picture was published elsewhere, that copy stands in for it, with a
note saying so. The pictures of embedded tweets appear under the tweet, also
with a note. Each article's comments are those of the most complete archived
copy of its page, so comments written after that copy are missing, and an
article whose archived pages hold no comments, or that has no archived page,
shows none. Links in the articles lead to their original pages, many of which no longer exist; each article also links to its
original address and the web archive capture it was restored from.
